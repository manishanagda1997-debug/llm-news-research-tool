# Summary judging guide, Part 7: an expectation and its outcome, and industry overreach

Parts 2 and 4 still apply to every item on sheet 2. This part covers the two questions of sheet 1. Each summary
appears under a letter only; nothing on the sheet or the page shows which prompt wrote it. Judge sheet 1 before
opening sheet 2.

## 1. Pairs (`summary_v2_p6_pairs.csv`)

A row names a summary and a pair of its articles: the first reports what is expected or an earlier stage of a
development, the second reports what then happens in the same development.

- **outlook_stated**: "yes" when one point states the expectation as an expectation and says who expects it (an
  analyst, a brokerage, the company, a poll). "no" when the expectation is missing, is written as a result, or has no
  source.
- **kept_apart**: "yes" only when the expectation and the outcome are in separate points, and the outcome is written
  as the outcome. "no" when they are merged into one point, when the outcome is written as something still
  expected, or when the outcome is missing.
- Judge only what the points say, with the articles on the page; the briefs do not count for this question.
- A short note is welcome; it is needed for every "no".

## 2. Industry overreach (`summary_v2_p6_overreach.csv`)

A row is one industry point of a summary, with the articles it cites.

- **overreach**: "yes" when the point extends the company's own results, sales, plans or deals to the sector, the
  market or other companies, beyond what the cited articles say about them. "no" when every claim about the sector,
  the market or other companies is in the cited articles.
- A "yes" needs a note that names the extended claim.
- Whether the point is supported at all is judged on sheet 2 with Parts 2 and 4; this question is only about
  extending the company's facts.

## 3. Order and lock

Sheet 1 (pairs, then overreach), then sheet 2 (items, then events). Every row gets an answer before the lock. The key
stays closed until the lock is made.
